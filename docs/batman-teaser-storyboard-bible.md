# Batman Anime Teaser Storyboard Bible

## Purpose

This document locks Sequence 01 of the teaser trailer: the Arkham blackout, Female Two-Face reveal, Batman fear reveal, first henchmen fight burst, and batarang button. This is not the full final scene, and it is not the entire teaser trailer. It is one selected teaser sequence that should sell mood, threat, Batman's tactical presence, Female Two-Face's duality, and the first burst of violent action without revealing every payoff from the final cut.

The sequence is a tribute to dark, operatic Batman cinema, with high-end Japanese action-anime rendering traits applied to an original Batman story direction. The rendering should feel sharp, kinetic, dramatic, and hand-composed. It should not make the story, world, or character designs look like another anime property.

## Creative North Star

An original Batman teaser set at Arkham, directed with heavy noir atmosphere, rain, strobing gunfire, hard silhouettes, and premium 2D anime action rendering. Batman should feel like a black armored knight emerging from the dark, not a superhero posing under clean lights.

The teaser is about fear and control:

- Female Two-Face controls the criminal procession until the lights fail.
- The goons try to control the dark with firearms.
- Batman controls the dark because he prepared for it.
- The night-vision close-up is a tactical escalation, not the first reveal.

## Scope

In scope for this teaser:

- Arkham exterior or semi-exterior location.
- Diverse group of painted goons.
- Female Two-Face leading them.
- Her hidden burned half revealed during the teaser.
- Blackout and Batman's first silhouette reveal.
- Later close-up with night-vision optics already active.
- Brutal non-lethal fight against armed henchmen.
- Gunfire, muzzle flashes, lightning, rain, and reflections as the visual rhythm.
- Batarang throw upward as the trailer button.

Held for the full release:

- Female Two-Face physically charging Batman.
- Batman knocking her away.
- Full explanation of her grief, motive, or origin.
- Any explicit depiction of how her burn happened.
- The full payoff of the batarang after the teaser button, unless a short result is needed for trailer punctuation.

## Location

Preferred location: Arkham transfer courtyard or exterior service exit.

This gives the scene:

- Rain and open sky for lightning.
- Wet concrete for reflected muzzle flashes.
- Arkham walls, barred exits, watch platforms, security gates, and brutal institutional architecture.
- Vertical height for the rifle threat above Batman.
- A believable reason for floodlights and security lights to cut out.

Do not lock the scene to a pure rooftop unless later staging demands it. The location can borrow rooftop mood, but the courtyard gives better geography for the teaser.

## Visual Rendering Rules

Use these traits:

- Premium gritty Japanese action-anime keyframe language.
- Sharp cel shading.
- Hard ink shadows.
- Rain drawn as graphic streaks, not soft mist.
- Muzzle flashes as white-orange impact frames.
- Red emergency light and red paint accents.
- Sparse lightning strikes for major silhouette beats.
- Speed smears, cape smears, and held impact frames.
- Heavy black negative space.
- Wet reflections on concrete, armor, guns, and the frame object.

Avoid:

- Directly copying any specific anime scene or character language.
- Making Batman look like an Attack on Titan character.
- Clean heroic superhero lighting.
- Constant lightning that feels fake.
- Overlit backgrounds.
- Photorealistic police-drama staging.
- Comedic fight choreography.
- Any ethnic caricature in the goon designs.

## Character Notes

### Batman

Batman is a mechanized but lightweight armored vigilante. His suit should feel inspired by tactical modern Batman armor: broad torso, short cowl ears, heavy cape mass, armored gauntlets, utility belt, reinforced boots, and a futuristic knight silhouette.

First appearance:

- No active glowing optics.
- No clean close-up of the eye technology.
- He reads as a black armored death-knight silhouette.
- The eye holes are present, but the image should not focus on them.
- The audience should feel the criminals see a shape, not a man.

Later tactical escalation:

- Jump to a close-up where night-vision optics are already active.
- Do not show the activation mechanism in this teaser.
- The optics help him read the darkness, the rifle threat above, and the batarang trajectory.
- The close-up should be backlit, darker, and cleaner than the existing reference image if that reference has too many background lights.

Combat code:

- Batman is brutal but non-lethal.
- He disarms, disables, breaks weapons, redirects bodies, uses cape control, and incapacitates.
- He does not casually murder armed men.
- The violence should feel frightening because of precision and speed, not gore.

### Female Two-Face

Working identity: Harvey Dent's wife, now functioning as Female Two-Face.

The teaser should reveal her duality visually:

- She appears first composed, formal, controlled, and partially hidden.
- She is blonde with medium shoulder-length hair.
- She appears late 30s to early 40s.
- She wears formal attire, elegant but severe.
- She holds what appears to be a frame.
- The camera initially favors one side of her face and body.
- Her burned half is carefully hidden by camera angle, hair, shadow, blocking, or the frame.
- A later flicker, lightning strike, muzzle flash, or camera shift reveals the burned half.

Backstory handling:

- Do not depict the origin of the burn.
- Do not explain or dramatize how the injury happened in the teaser.
- The trailer only needs the visual duality and emotional implication.

Performance:

- She is not played as a joke.
- She is dangerous because she is composed, grieving, obsessive, and theatrical.
- Her authority over the goons should be clear before Batman interrupts the scene.

### Goons

The goons should be a diverse criminal crew, including white, Black, Hispanic, Indian/South Asian, and other plausible American urban backgrounds. The group can be majority white in a way that feels natural to the setting, but representation should be visible and respectful.

Design rules:

- Avoid linking ethnicity to role, stupidity, danger, or cowardice.
- Give the group a shared criminal visual identity through paint, clothing, weapons, and behavior.
- Some have a red trail or stripe on one side.
- Others are painted half-and-half.
- The paint should echo Female Two-Face's split identity without becoming clownish.
- They may carry automatic firearms and handguns.

## Lighting Rules

The scene should not rely on constant lightning.

Lighting hierarchy:

1. Arkham security lights and floodlights before the blackout.
2. Sudden blackout in the immediate action zone.
3. Muzzle flashes as the main action lighting.
4. Occasional lightning for major silhouette beats.
5. Red emergency glows and wet reflections for continuity.
6. Batman's night-vision optics later as a tactical close-up element.

Gunfire is crucial. It creates believable strobing light and lets the fight read in fragments:

- Flash: Batman appears behind a gunman.
- Flash: the gun is knocked aside.
- Flash: the goon hits the ground.
- Darkness: cape motion and rain.
- Flash: another attacker fires into empty space.

## Teaser Beat Map

### Beat 1: Arkham Procession

The teaser opens on an Arkham transfer courtyard or exterior service exit in heavy rain. Security lights and floodlights carve out wet concrete, gates, and institutional walls. The painted goons move in a controlled group. Some carry firearms. Their faces and clothes show red streaks or half-and-half paint.

Female Two-Face leads them. She is seen from one side, elegant and severe, carrying a frame. Her hidden side is not yet visible.

### Beat 2: Duality Introduction

The camera favors her composed side. Her voice, posture, or stillness suggests control. A subtle change in angle, lighting, or reflection hints that another side is being withheld.

The teaser may show the frame reflecting a distorted part of her face, but it should not reveal too much too early.

### Beat 3: Burned Half Reveal

A flicker, lightning strike, or muzzle-flash-like light reveals the burned half. This is the first true Female Two-Face image for the teaser.

The reveal should be striking but not exploitative. It should communicate duality, grief, and danger. Do not explain the origin.

### Beat 4: Blackout

The lights cut out across the immediate zone. The courtyard does not become perfectly invisible, but it becomes frighteningly dark. The goons react and raise weapons.

Female Two-Face freezes. Her authority is suddenly challenged by something she cannot see.

### Beat 5: Batman Silhouette

In a flash of lightning or gunfire, Batman is visible as a black armored death-knight silhouette. His optics are not active yet. The audience does not get the clean tech close-up here.

This is the fear image.

### Beat 6: First Burst Of Gunfire

The goons fire into the dark. The muzzle flashes create the first rhythm of the fight. Batman is never where they expect him to be.

Use close and medium shots:

- Gauntlet knocks a rifle upward.
- Cape absorbs or hides movement.
- Boot steps through a puddle.
- A goon fires into rain and darkness.
- Batman's arm enters frame and ends the shot.

### Beat 7: Night-Vision Close-Up

Jump to Batman's face in close-up. The night-vision optics are already active. The background is dark and backlit, with fewer distracting lights than earlier concept/reference images.

This beat tells the audience: the dark belongs to him now.

### Beat 8: Raw Henchmen Fight

The fight escalates. The teaser should show fragments, not full choreography:

- Batman pivots through automatic fire.
- A rifle is bent, broken, or stripped away.
- A henchman is thrown into another without lethal impact.
- Cape arcs through muzzle flashes.
- Rain explodes off armor.
- Red paint streaks smear under harsh flashes.
- Lightning shows the full silhouette once or twice, sparingly.

### Beat 9: Threat Above

Batman reads a threat above: a rifle, thin red laser, or elevated shooter on an Arkham platform, tower, or upper level. Others cannot clearly see it in the darkness.

His active optics justify how he tracks it.

### Beat 10: Batarang Trailer Button

Batman throws the batarang upward. The camera follows it in slow motion through rain, smoke, muzzle-flash residue, and red laser haze.

End on the batarang or bat-ring result as the trailer button. The exact payoff can remain partial, as long as it feels sharp and deliberate.

## Prompt Language

Use concrete rendering traits instead of direct style-copy language:

```text
premium gritty Japanese action anime keyframe, rain-soaked Arkham transfer courtyard at night, hard cel shading, sharp ink linework, black armored vigilante with short cowl ears and massive cape, dramatic negative space, wet concrete reflections, muzzle flash lighting, sparse lightning, red emergency glow, cinematic noir atmosphere
```

Batman first reveal prompt anchor:

```text
black armored vigilante appears as a death-knight silhouette in a burst of lightning and gunfire, no glowing eye optics yet, heavy cape mass, broad tactical armor, short cowl ears, rain streaks, hard cel shadows, terrifying stillness
```

Night-vision close-up prompt anchor:

```text
close-up of black armored vigilante cowl, night-vision optics already active, backlit in rain, background almost black with minimal lights, sharp cel shading, hard ink shadows, tactical intensity, no activation mechanism shown
```

Female Two-Face reveal prompt anchor:

```text
formal blonde crime leader in late 30s or early 40s, elegant severe posture, one side of face hidden by shadow and framing, later flash reveals burned half, rain-soaked Arkham courtyard, tragic duality, controlled menace, cinematic anime rendering, respectful non-exploitative framing
```

Goons prompt anchor:

```text
diverse criminal crew in rain, white Black Hispanic Indian and other backgrounds, shared red and half-face paint markings, tactical street clothing, firearms raised, no ethnic caricature, wet concrete, muzzle flash strobe lighting, gritty anime action keyframe
```

## Generation Safety And Credit Rules

Before any paid generation:

1. Confirm exact shot count.
2. Confirm which shots need still keyframes first.
3. Generate one still test only.
4. Review silhouette, lighting, Batman suit read, Female Two-Face read, and goon representation.
5. Only batch generate after the test passes.
6. Avoid video generation until still keyframes are approved.

If a generator cannot preserve Batman's silhouette, the burned-half reveal, or readable action in darkness, stop and switch approach rather than spending more credits.

## Immediate Production Instruction

Produce Sequence 01 as a cut sheet first, then render from that cut sheet. The cut sheet must specify exact shots, timing, camera, lighting, character action, required keyframes, render prompt, and review criteria.

Do not run paid video generation until the Sequence 01 still keyframes are approved. The first render pass should be a small keyframe set, not a full batch of moving clips.
